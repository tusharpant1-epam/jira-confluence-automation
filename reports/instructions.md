# How to Complete a Project Status Report

Use [template.md](./template.md) for each reporting period. Keep the report concise, factual, and useful to someone who needs to understand project health or take action.

## Before you start

1. Make a copy of the template for the project and reporting period; keep the template unchanged.
2. Set the project name, reporting period, report date, and accountable owner.
3. Use a consistent date format (`YYYY-MM-DD`) and reporting cadence for the project.

## Fill in each section

- **Overall status:** Choose one status and explain it in the summary.
  - **Green:** Work is expected to meet agreed scope and milestones; no material issue needs escalation.
  - **Amber:** A risk or dependency could affect delivery, and active mitigation or a decision is needed.
  - **Red:** A material issue is already affecting, or is likely to prevent, an agreed outcome or milestone without intervention.
- **Summary:** Write 2–4 sentences covering overall health, notable change this period, and the most important attention item. Avoid unexplained jargon.
- **Progress this period:** List outcomes completed during the reporting period, not just activity. Include dates or evidence when useful.
- **Plans for next period:** List a small set of specific, verifiable priorities. Add an owner and target date when known.
- **Risks and blockers:** Describe the potential or actual impact, the response, an accountable owner, and a due date. Distinguish a risk (may happen) from a blocker (already preventing progress). If there are none, state “None identified” and remove the unused table row.
- **Decisions or help needed:** State the exact decision or support, who needs to provide it, and by when. If none, write “None.”
- **Milestones:** Show the agreed target date and current status. Use “At risk” when a milestone may slip and explain why in Risks and blockers.

## Review before sharing

- Confirm dates, owners, status, and progress with the people responsible.
- Make sure the summary agrees with the status and the details below it.
- Do not present estimates, assumptions, or sample data as confirmed facts. Label them clearly.
- Remove bracketed prompts and any sections or table rows that do not apply.
- Escalate urgent blockers through the team's normal channel rather than relying on the report alone.
